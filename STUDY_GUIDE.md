# Studying with Danya

This independent guide helps readers find Daniel Naroditsky's teaching and put it into practice. The selection and study activities are the repository editor's work. They are not a curriculum written or approved by Danya or his family. Sources checked September 4, 2026.

## Start with what he explicitly valued

These are supported examples, not a claim to rank everything he taught. The articles below already appear in the Chess.com ZIP; this guide makes their connection to learning easier to find.

| Theme | What the source supports | Read or watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Learn from your own mistakes | In his own account, reflecting on losses and extracting lessons was his most important technique for improvement. This is his assessment, not a promised result for every learner. | [The Chess Investigator: Analyze Your Mistakes](./Links/README.md#l018), paragraph beginning “Neither masochistic tendencies…” |
| Build positional understanding | He connected his development to sustained positional study and keeping a notebook, and emphasized the value of positional understanding alongside endgame technique. | [Introduction to My Friday Columns](./Links/README.md#l019), his account of the 2006 notebook |
| Study endgames through both explanation and analysis | In his first column, he explains why he will emphasize practical endings and combine verbal explanation with calculation. | [Blazing A Path](./The_Practical_Endgame_2014-2020.pdf#page=1), June 2014, printed pp. 46-47; PDF pp. 1-2 |
| Train through composed studies | He describes benefits to visualization, awareness of opposing resources, and imagination from his own study-solving. | [Why Solving Studies Is So Important](./Links/README.md#l020) |
| Work actively with an engine | He recommends analyzing independently first, questioning engine suggestions, and understanding why moves work. The article describes software in 2016; its technical comparisons are historical. | [How To Use A Chess Computer](./Links/README.md#l021) |

There is also direct support for prioritizing his endgame videos. The description on his own [King Activity video](./Links/README.md#l022) introduces a series on “the most important topics within positional chess and endgames.” This is a quotation from the upload description, not a transcribed spoken quotation. ## A practical way to use the collection

The following routine is an editorial suggestion, not a quotation or prescribed schedule from Danya.

1. **Choose one lesson.** Start with the King Activity video, a column in the [endgame index](./PRACTICAL_ENDGAME_INDEX.md), or the article about learning from mistakes.
2. **Make your own attempt.** At a position that interests you, pause and write down your candidate move, the opponent's strongest reply, and your reasoning.
3. **Compare explanations.** Read or watch the author's analysis. Distinguish the game's actual moves from suggested variations. Record what changed your understanding.
4. **Check a game of your own.** Find a related decision, analyze it independently, then use an engine or tablebase where appropriate. Keep the source's original analysis separate from later corrections.
5. **Leave a useful note.** Use the [study-note template](./STUDY_NOTES_TEMPLATE.md) to capture one lesson you can recognize in another game.

You do not need to finish a playlist or reach a particular rating before learning from an individual example. The order here is a convenient starting point, not an ability assessment.
