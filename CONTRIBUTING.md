# Adding to this collection

The purpose of this independent tribute is to help people find and learn from Daniel Naroditsky's work while preserving accurate attribution.

## What makes a useful addition

Add an original teaching source, a carefully checked index, or a short study aid that helps readers use an existing source. Explain what the addition contributes and whether it is Danya's work, another person's commentary, or your own interpretation.

For each addition, record:

- The title, author or speaker, and original host.
- The source URL and date checked.
- The publication or upload date, if verified; distinguish it from a later update date.
- A precise location for the claim: printed page, PDF page, section, or video timestamp.
- What was checked and what remains uncertain.

## Preserve his voice accurately

Use quotation marks only for wording checked against the original. Label paraphrases as paraphrases. For audio, check the recording before treating automatic captions or a third-party transcript as an exact quotation. Do not combine excerpts into a new statement or attribute an editor's foreword to Danya.

If describing a teaching as something he especially valued, supply a source in which he expresses that judgment. Popularity and another person's praise do not establish his own priorities.

Identify posthumous editing and the contributors to annotated games. Label your own exercises, study sequences, and corrections as your own work. Keep tributes and personal recollections attributed to their speakers.

## Sources and corrections

Prefer links to original uploads and publisher pages. Record permission or license information when available; do not describe material as openly licensed simply because it is freely readable. Keep source credit for artwork and photographs.

If a factual or chess-analysis claim is uncertain, leave it clearly marked as unverified or omit it until checked. A modern engine correction should identify the position, engine or tablebase, and result, and remain separate from the historical annotation. Do not silently rewrite an author's words.

Keep the collection focused on teaching and remembrance. Claims about private circumstances or disputes do not belong in a study guide without a separate, well-supported reason for including them. Use respectful language about Danya, his family, and the people in his examples.

Report corrections with the affected entry and supporting source. The repository's independent status should remain clear; no family or institutional endorsement should be implied.
